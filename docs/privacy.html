Privacy Policy

Effective November 1, 2026

wordsword is a dictionary and vocabulary app. It keeps your data on your iPhone. This policy explains the few things that leave the device, and why.

1. What stays on your device

Everything you build in wordsword is stored on your iPhone. None of it is uploaded to us:

  • Words you save, and the definitions, examples, pronunciations and synonyms attached to them
  • Your wordlists and word chains
  • Your flashcard review history and schedule
  • Your recent searches
  • Your game progress: Word Wheel levels and coins, Linking words results and stats
  • Your username

We don't run a server that receives this information, so we can't see it. Deleting the app removes all of it.

2. Your account

An account is optional. Everything in wordsword works without one. All an account does is set a username.

Accounts use Sign in with Apple. When you sign in, Apple gives wordsword an anonymous identifier for your account. The first time only, it also gives your first name, if you choose to share it. We use the name only to suggest a username, and we don't ask Apple for your email address. Both the identifier and the username stay on your iPhone. Apple handles sign-in under Apple's privacy policy.

3. What's sent when you look up a word

To define a word, wordsword sends the word you looked up to two third-party services:

ServiceWhat's sentWhy
Free Dictionary API (dictionaryapi.dev)The wordDefinitions, examples, pronunciation audio
Datamuse (api.datamuse.com)The wordSynonyms, spelling suggestions, pronunciation

When you play a pronunciation, your phone downloads the audio file for that word from the Free Dictionary API.

These requests contain only the word. They don't include your name, account, username, or any identifier from us. Like any web request, they do include ordinary network information such as your IP address. We never send these services your library or your history, and we don't ask them to link lookups to you. Words already in your library are shown from your phone. At most, one is sent once more to fetch a missing pronunciation.

4. Explanations written on your phone

On iPhones that support Apple Intelligence, wordsword uses Apple's on-device language model to rewrite definitions in plain English and to "Explain it differently". The model runs entirely on your iPhone, so the word and the text it writes never leave it. On other iPhones, wordsword uses the dictionary's own wording.

5. Voice input

If you tap the mic in the search field and say a word, wordsword uses Apple's speech recognition to turn your speech into text.

Where that happens depends on your iPhone. If your iPhone supports on-device speech recognition, the audio never leaves it, and wordsword always uses on-device recognition when it can. Otherwise, the recorded audio is sent to Apple to be transcribed, and Apple's privacy policy covers that.

The microphone is on only after you tap the mic. It stops when you stop talking or tap again. The audio is used only to find the word you said. We never store recordings, and audio goes nowhere except Apple's speech recognition, as described above. You can say no to microphone and speech recognition access, or turn it off later in iOS Settings, and keep using wordsword by typing.

6. Sharing

When you share a Linking words result, wordsword gives the text to the iOS share sheet. It goes only where you choose to send it.

7. What we don't do

  • We don't use analytics or crash-reporting tools.
  • We don't show ads, and we don't track you across apps or websites.
  • We don't build a profile about you.
  • We don't sell or share your personal data.
  • We don't knowingly collect personal information from children.

8. Keeping and deleting your data

Your library lives on your iPhone, so it's yours to keep or delete:

  • Delete your account: Settings → You → Delete account. This permanently removes your account identifier and username from your iPhone. Your saved words stay unless you clear them too. To also remove wordsword from your Apple ID, go to iOS Settings → your name → Sign in with Apple → wordsword, then choose to stop using it.
  • Delete your words: Settings → Clear all my words. This removes every saved word and wordlist.
  • Delete everything: delete the app, and all its data goes with it.
  • Take back a permission: turn off microphone or speech recognition access at any time in iOS Settings → wordsword.

We never receive your library or your account, so we have no copies to keep. The services in section 3 receive the words you look up, and their own policies say how long they keep them: Free Dictionary API, Datamuse, Apple.

9. Changes

If this policy changes in a meaningful way, we'll update the date at the top and mention the change in the app's release notes.
