# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Gen Z and millennial readers. Context: mid-book (couch, bed, train), one hand on the book, phone in the other. They hit a word they don't know, want to understand it in ~3 seconds and get back to the page — and want that word to still be there next week, not lost in browser history.

## Product Purpose

wordsword is a dictionary + thesaurus that *keeps* your words. Type a word → get a plain-English definition in a contained, tap-only chat (no free-text input; three suggested follow-ups). Every looked-up word lands in your library automatically; you can file words into **wordlists** (Spotify playlists, but for words), review them as spaced-repetition flashcards, and wander synonym chains. Success = the user actually remembers the words they looked up.

## Brand Personality

Doodle · whimsical · sharp. Feels like notes in a good notebook in high school circa 2010 — but with a modern, precise UI. A smart friend who defines a word in one sentence and doesn't lecture. Voice: short, plain, warm, never cute-for-cute's-sake.

## Anti-references

- Dictionary.com / Merriam-Webster apps: dense, ad-laden, every sense at once.
- Duolingo-style gamification: no mascots nagging, no streaks-as-guilt.
- Cream/parchment "old book" skins. The notebook feel comes from ink, ruled hairlines, hand-drawn strokes and typography — not a beige background.
- Generic "AI chat app" UI: no free-text composer, no avatars, no typing dots theater.

## Design Principles

1. **Three seconds, then back to the book.** The input is the home page. Nothing competes with it.
2. **Always show what's next.** Every screen offers exactly the next 1–3 taps. No dead ends, no blank composer.
3. **Words are songs, wordlists are playlists.** Saving is one tap and never blocks reading the definition.
4. **Ink marks meaning.** Color and motion only appear to mark the word, a state change, or feedback — never as decoration.
5. **Earned familiarity.** Standard iOS affordances (nav, sheets, lists); personality lives in type, strokes and the word chain — not in reinvented controls.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Dynamic Type throughout; ≥4.5:1 text contrast in light and dark; every animation has a reduced-motion crossfade; VoiceOver labels on icon-only buttons; color never the only signal (flashcard results use icon + label).
